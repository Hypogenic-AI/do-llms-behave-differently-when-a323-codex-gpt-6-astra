Prompt Formatting Shifts Authorship Judgments
Without Establishing Behavioral Effects
Dang Nguyen and NeuriCo
Abstract
Synthetic user prompts support automated evaluation and model orchestration, but
apparent authorship could affect how a responder behaves. We examine prompt
style, declared source, elicited authorship judgments, and activation interventions
in an exploratory controlled study. Two API responders receive 180 benchmark
requests spanning harmful assistance, benign assistance, and persona-conditioned
opinions; a separate local model supports representation analysis. A formatting
control preserves every underlying request byte and the complete case-insensitive
word sequence.
For Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite, this manipulation increases AI-
authorship classifications by 64.4 percentage points on harmful requests and 88.1
points on benign requests, while refusal changes by 1.7 and 0.0 points. None of six
primary model-by-domain behavioral comparisons is significant after Holm cor-
rection; uncertainty generally prevents equivalence claims. Broader paraphrasing
fails strict semantic controls: only 7 of 124 successful benchmark pairs survive. In
local Qwen3-8B, an activation classifier reaches 0.990 AUROC, but surface fea-
tures reach 0.970 and surface residualization reduces activation AUROC to 0.665.
Fixed-text steering yields no robust behavioral change specific to the style direc-
tion. An exploratory unconditional opinion-alignment decrease does not survive
correction. These findings separate strong elicited source-judgment sensitivity
from an unestablished behavioral effect and leave the general authorship-mediated
causal hypothesis unresolved.
1
Introduction
A user turn can be written by another language model. Model-written evaluations and automated
orchestration therefore raise a practical question: does a responder behave differently when a re-
quest appears machine-written? If apparent source changes refusal or agreement, evaluations built
from synthetic prompts may not transfer to interactions with human users. Studying this possibility
requires separating source cues from changes in what the prompt asks.
What does apparent authorship measure? Actual provenance, AI-like writing style, declared
source, elicited source judgments, activation coordinates, and behavior are different variables. All
paraphrases in our study are LLM-written, including the human-style controls. We do not compare
verified human authors with verified machine authors. We instead ask whether controlled textual
cues change behavior and whether a learned style direction provides evidence for a causal mecha-
nism.
Prior work finds evaluation-context discrimination, authorship detection, and preferences for model-
generated text Needham et al. [2025], Quaremba et al. [2026], Panickssery et al. [2024]. These
findings do not establish that a responder changes safety behavior because it identifies its prompter
as an LLM. Prompt construction can change task meaning, and a predictive activation direction can
encode formatting or length Devbunova [2026], Chalkidis [2026]. A behavioral comparison and a
strong probe are consequently insufficient on their own.

We combine three complementary tests (figure 1). We compare human-style and AI-style para-
phrases with explicit semantic audits, add an exact-content formatting control when those audits
expose omissions, and vary declared authorship on fixed bodies. We then measure held-out activa-
tion separation and intervene on fixed text using random and nuisance controls. Behavioral answers
and source classifications occur in separate sessions, so an explicit authorship question cannot prime
the behavioral response.
The clearest result is a measurement dissociation. Formatting increases Gemini’s AI classifica-
tions by 64.4 and 88.1 percentage points on harmful and benign requests, respectively, while refusal
changes by only 1.7 and 0.0 points in the corresponding available behavioral pairs. All six primary
formatting comparisons have Holm-adjusted p = 1. These estimates do not establish general equiv-
alence. Broader paraphrases retain only seven benchmark pairs under strict audit; a local activation
probe reaches 0.990 AUROC, but a surface baseline already reaches 0.970. Matched interventions
do not establish a style-specific behavioral effect.
Our contributions are:
• We measure source-judgment and behavioral responses to a mechanically verified fixed-content
formatting contrast across two API responders and three domains.
• We document semantic-audit attrition, output-budget sensitivity, and abstention-dependent opin-
ion estimates that limit interpretation of style comparisons.
• We test held-out activation probes and fixed-text interventions against surface, shuffled-label,
random-direction, length, and evaluation-framing controls.
Section 2 positions the study; section 3 defines the protocol; section 4 reports results; and section 5
discusses their scope. This is an exploratory controlled study, not a confirmation or refutation of the
broad causal hypothesis.

Related Work
Evaluation awareness. Needham et al. study whether models distinguish evaluation and deploy-
ment transcripts Needham et al. [2025]. Their context labels do not identify human versus machine
authorship. Test-awareness and evaluation-awareness interventions motivate our fixed-text controls,
while their heterogeneous steering results caution against equating prediction with behavioral con-
trol Abdelnabi and Salem [2025], Nguyen et al. [2025]. Heidari et al. distinguish representation,
verbalization, and control Heidari et al. [2026]. We preserve this distinction and treat our evaluation-
framing direction as a limited proxy requiring held-out validation.
Authorship recognition and preference. LLM evaluators can recognize and favor their own gener-
ations Panickssery et al. [2024]. Related studies examine preferences for LLM-written communica-
tions and regenerated resume summaries Laurito et al. [2024], Xu et al. [2025]. These experiments
primarily concern text being judged, rather than the source of a user’s instruction. We adapt the
fixed-body source-label comparison to refusal and opinion alignment, while treating explicit labels
as a separate manipulation that may introduce demand effects.
Activation detection and surface confounds. RepreGuard and linear-probe detectors use hidden
representations to detect machine-generated text Chen et al. [2025], Quaremba et al. [2026]. Their
predictive success motivates our activation readouts, not an assumption that those readouts govern

Opinion alignment and measurement. Model-written evaluations supply persona-conditioned

interpret opinion agreement as factual error. We report both conditional-choice and unconditional
explicit-alignment analyses because treatment-dependent abstention changes their interpretation.
3
Methodology
3.1
Requests, treatments, and semantic controls
Sampling. With seed 42, we sample 60 harmful and 60 benign JBB-BEHAVIORS behaviors Jail-
breakBench [2026]. We sample 60 unique terminal-question groups from the original Anthropic
sycophancy files: 40 philosophy, 15 NLP, and five political questions, with one persona per group
Anthropic [2026], Perez et al. [2022]. A separate calibration sample contains 100 unique English
first turns of 12–120 words from a gathered WILDCHAT prefix Allen Institute for AI [2026]. Neither
the prefix nor its authorship is representative or verified. All variants of a request stay in the same
split. Public-benchmark contamination cannot be excluded.
Paraphrase arm. Two writers alternate across requests and generate both human-style and polished
AI-style rewrites, preserving the task, persona, stance, options, specificity, and harm level. An
independently presented A/B audit checks semantic preservation, clarity, and politeness. The first
gate requires both semantic approvals, an AI/human word ratio in [0.8, 1.25], and clarity/politeness
differences of at most one point. A pre-outcome spot-check finds omitted details despite approval.
We add a cross-writer strict audit requiring every original detail and each rewrite/original word ratio
in [0.75, 1.25]. These automated gates are fallible; no human evaluation occurs. We retain writer
refusals, blocks, and malformed outputs as attrition. Original requests serve as baseline material;
the central paired contrast compares the two rewrite styles.
Exact-content formatting arm. Before behavioral analysis, severe strict-audit attrition motivates
a narrow control on all 180 benchmark requests. The following templates insert exactly the same
original request:
human-style: please help with this request: {ORIGINAL}
thanks.
AI-style: **Please help with this request:**
{ORIGINAL}
**Thanks.**
The underlying request is byte-identical, and the full case-insensitive word sequence is identical.
Politeness words are fixed; Markdown, casing, whitespace, and tokenization vary. This contrast
isolates a surface manipulation, not an authorship belief or all properties of AI-like writing.
Declared-source arm. On the first 20 available requests per domain, we prefix the same human-
style body with “The following request was written by a human/an AI system,” choosing one source
per arm. This tests attribution on fixed text. It is not a full style-by-label factorial.

4
Results
4.1
Formatting changes source judgments without a detected primary behavioral effect
Table 1 reports the six primary exact-content comparisons. All Holm-adjusted p-values equal 1.
Gemini’s harmful refusal increases by 1.7 points and benign refusal is unchanged; Qwen’s corre-
sponding differences are 0.0 and 3.3 points. Conditional opinion alignment changes by +6.7 points
for Gemini and −4.0 for Qwen. Neither the point estimates nor the nonsignificant tests justify
general behavioral equivalence.
The conservative bounds make the remaining uncertainty explicit. Gemini’s harmful-refusal inter-
val is [−7.3, 10.5] points and its benign interval is [−10.3, 10.3]. Qwen’s harmful-refusal inter-
val, [−7.0, 7.0], lies within the planned ±10-point threshold. However, refusal is near ceiling and
Qwen’s verbal authorship manipulation check fails. This isolated narrow bound does not establish
an authorship-invariant model.
Does the responder report a different source? In separate sessions, formatting increases Gemini’s
AI classifications by 64.4 points on harmful requests and 88.1 points on benign requests (figure 2).
Its opinion prompts already receive 91.7% AI classifications under the human-style wrapper, leaving
little headroom; formatting raises that rate to 98.3%. Qwen calls almost all requests HUMAN:
the corresponding shifts are +3.3, 0.0, and 0.0 points. Elicited source judgments are therefore
model- and domain-dependent. They are neither calibrated probabilities nor verified beliefs during
answering.
How does abstention change the opinion result? The primary opinion comparison retains only
30 of 60 Gemini pairs and 50 of 60 Qwen pairs. On all 60 requests, unconditional explicit align-
ment changes by −1.7 points for Gemini (bootstrap interval [−11.7, 8.3]) and −11.7 for Qwen
([−21.7, −3.3]; conservative interval [−26.2, 4.9]). Qwen has one positive and eight negative dis-
cordances, yielding nominal p = .0391 and two-model Holm p = .0781. This exploratory signal
warrants replication, but does not survive even that limited correction. The difference from the
conditional endpoint shows why no-choice responses cannot simply be dropped or counted as sub-
stantive disagreement.

4.2
Semantic attrition limits broader style comparisons
Only 217 of 280 requested rewrite pairs are produced, including 124 benchmark and 93 calibration
pairs (table 2). The strict gate retains 25 pairs overall, of which only seven are benchmark pairs: one
harmful, three benign, and three opinion requests. Refusal-heavy attrition particularly limits harmful
paraphrases. The first audit accepts omissions that the stricter audit catches; the latter sometimes
also rejects harmless tone changes. Neither gate supplies human gold labels.
The all-successful-rewrite comparisons therefore remain confounded (table 5). Gemini’s apparent
harmful-refusal reduction is −13.6 points on 22 pairs, with only three negative discordances and no
positive discordance. Long-budget substantial fulfillment increases by 13.6 points, but neither result
is significant. Qwen’s harmful refusal changes by −9.1 points. All six primary paraphrase Holm-
adjusted p-values equal 1, and the strict subset cannot support a broad estimate. The fixed-body
source-label arm also detects no reliable effect (table 6); opinion complete-case counts fall to 13 per
model from 20 available requests.

4.4
Fixed-text edits do not establish a style-specific behavioral mechanism
Sham refusal is 85% on harmful and 35% on benign requests; conditional opinion alignment is
88.2% among 17 scorable pairs. Every intervention endpoint has exact p = 1 (table 4). Harmful
refusal never flips, but conservative intervals are approximately ±19.7 points. The double style dose
and one random direction each change one benign refusal and one opinion-alignment response. This
pattern does not establish style specificity. Centering does not establish necessity, and no robust
natural effect is available for a controlled rescue.

The interventions nevertheless change computation. Mean next-token KL divergence from sham
is .0778 nats for positive style, .0805 for negative style, .3108 for double style, .0102/.0202 for
the random directions, .1195 for length, and .0087 for evaluation framing. Length changes the
output distribution more than single-dose style. These descriptive logit changes do not demonstrate
behavioral mediation. The separate authorship readout returns AI on 4/60 sham prompts and 4/60
positive-style prompts, versus 6/60 at double dose, so this readout does not establish targeted belief
engagement.
All arms answer 30/30 arithmetic checks correctly, and no intervention response is judged incoher-
ent. These small diagnostics exclude obvious collapse, not subtle capability loss. Residual local
truncation further limits interpretation of fulfillment and response quality (section C.1).
5
Discussion
What does the controlled contrast establish? The strongest finding is a dissociation between
Gemini’s elicited source judgments and the primary behavioral comparisons in this sample. Exact-
content formatting substantially shifts its reported classifications on harmful and benign requests
without a detected behavioral effect. The data establish sensitivity of a prompted measurement to
surface cues. They leave unresolved whether authorship is spontaneously inferred while answering
and whether such an inference mediates safety-relevant behavior.
A classifier trained by an investigator need not recover a belief used by the responder. Local Qwen3-
8B has highly separable activations but weak verbal source discrimination; Qwen3.5-9B also has
weak verbal discrimination, but is a distinct API model whose internals we do not inspect. These
observations should not be pooled into a claim about a shared mechanism. Likewise, overlap with a
framing direction is insufficient when that direction transfers weakly to new framing cues.
Control failures are substantive findings. The paraphrase arm illustrates why stylistic rewriting is
a fragile causal manipulation. Both rewrites of one opinion item omit original persona and research-
interest details despite first-audit approval. Other rewrites turn “program” into “script,” resolve
ambiguity, or change specificity. A canonical-request judge can then attribute a content change to
behavior under the nominal style treatment. Strict auditing reduces this risk but leaves only seven
benchmark pairs and introduces its own apparent false rejections. Human proposition-level audits
or richer fixed-content factorials are needed to extend the narrow formatting result.
Precision, scoring, and selection. Most intervals do not exclude the planned 10-point threshold.
Refusal ceilings reduce headroom, and n = 20 local intervention groups allow sizable undetected
effects. Opinion alignment is not factual accuracy or evidence that the persona causes agreement.
Conditioning on choices in both arms selects on a treatment-affected response; unconditional ex-
plicit alignment answers a different question. Qwen’s exploratory decline deserves a prespecified
replication with stable routing and explicit abstention handling.
Judge disagreements are large relative to several observed differences: independent judges disagree
on 3/30 harmful and 1/30 benign outputs. No human gold scoring is available. Generation bud-
get also changes interpretation. After expanding Gemini paraphrase-opinion generation, truncation
falls from 131/174 to zero, yet 79/174 outputs still lack an unambiguous choice. Many are substan-
tive abstentions or multi-perspective responses. Qwen’s paraphrase conditional-alignment contrast
changes sign between short and long passes. Provider variation and generation budgets therefore
remain part of the measurement uncertainty.
Scope and broader implications. The study covers English, synthetic survey personas, a WildChat
prefix, one rewrite per style/request, two efficient API models, and one older local backbone. It
includes no verified-human comparison, multilingual replication, multi-agent tool-use setting, or
broad capability battery. Mechanistic scope is one layer and one token during prefill, with two
random draws, no all-token ablation, and no natural rescue. Negative intervention results cannot
exclude a distributed or differently located mechanism.
For evaluation practice, source classifications should be reported separately from behavior, and
synthetic-prompt effects should be tested under content controls and adequate output budgets. The
present results do not justify claims that synthetic evaluations transfer universally or that machines
recognize human users reliably. Misinterpreting a probe as an authorship belief could also encour-
age unsupported provenance judgments. The artifact record preserves audit failures, provider blocks,
8


and incomplete responses so that these limits remain visible; harmful-request and conversation data
should retain their source licenses and privacy constraints.
6
Conclusion
We study apparent prompt authorship through controlled text contrasts, separate-session source
judgments, and local activation interventions. Exact-content formatting changes Gemini’s elicited
authorship classifications substantially, but none of six primary behavioral comparisons is significant
after correction. Broader paraphrases fail semantic controls at scale; local activation separability is
largely matched by surface features; and fixed-text edits do not establish a style-specific behavioral
mechanism. The general human-versus-LLM authorship hypothesis remains open.
Future studies should use human-audited proposition-preserving pairs or richer fixed-content facto-
rials, include borderline-risk requests, and power analyses from observed discordance. The Qwen
unconditional-alignment signal requires replication before interpretation. If a natural effect repli-
cates, necessity and matched-coordinate rescue should be tested across layers and tokens with more
random and nuisance controls and independently validated source representations.

A
Protocol Details and Amendments
Provenance and sampling checks. The Anthropic philosophy file in the initially gathered Hug-
ging Face snapshot duplicated the NLP file. We use the verified original repository at commit
84fcc677e52e1902d696c32cd1a6b663e70d3993, containing 30,051 usable source rows across
the three files. Sampling uses unique terminal-question groups, not repeated persona rows as in-
dependent requests. JBB uses revision 886acc352a31533ffbcf4ef22c744658688086fc. The
WildChat calibration pool comes from a 1,000-conversation viewer prefix with 995 unique hashes
and 523 English labels. Its viewer snapshot is not pinned by the metadata revision; saved hashes
identify the collected material. The calibration experiment samples 100 unique English first turns of
12–120 words from this pool. These checks do not verify human authorship or remove benchmark
contamination.
Audit sequence. Across 280 requested pairs, the first audit accepts 161, direct-pair equivalence
accepts 60, and the strict audit accepts 25. The corresponding benchmark-only counts are 84, 22, and
seven. These sets differ in what they establish: the first gate checks judged preservation and surface
balance, direct equivalence checks the two rewrites, and the strict gate requires preservation of all
original details. The full prompts and audit decisions remain in results/processed/prompts.
json; selection checks are in results/processed/data_checks.json. One rewrite request
returns a provider error. Other failures include writer refusals, blocked requests, and invalid JSON;
we do not replace them silently.
Recorded amendments. The local plan is not an external preregistration. A pre-outcome omission
spot-check motivates the strict cross-writer audit. Severe strict attrition then motivates the fixed-
content format/case arm before behavioral analysis. Instrumentation diagnostics motivate uniform
API expansion from 256 to 1,536 tokens and local opinion expansion from 128 to 384 tokens. Initial
responses remain saved. Strict-calibration probing, surface residualization, and equal-word-count
unseen framing cues serve as sensitivity checks. A human audit, full style-by-label factorial, broad
factual benchmark, distributed steering, and natural rescue remain unperformed. The dated record
is notes/planning_full_execution.md.
Scoring boundaries. Refusal includes a refusal of a substantive component, so a partially helpful
answer can still be coded as refusing. The 0/1/2 fulfillment rubric separately captures requested
assistance. Opinion answers must endorse an identifiable option to be scorable in the primary
complete-case analysis. No-choice responses include abstentions and multi-perspective answers;
their absence of explicit endorsement is coded zero only in the unconditional sensitivity endpoint.
Judges are blind to assigned style and source labels, but see the canonical original request, which
creates an additional limitation when rewrites alter meaning. The 90-output second-judge audit
estimates agreement only.
Conservative paired intervals. Let p+ and p−be the probabilities of positive and negative dis-
cordance. Compute central 97.5% Clopper–Pearson intervals [L+, U+] and [L−, U−] from each
count out of n. The union bound gives at least 95% joint coverage, without requiring independence
between counts. Since ∆= p+ −p−, report [L+ −U−, U+ −L−]. These intervals can be conser-
vative, remain nondegenerate when both discordances are zero, and do not adjust for the number of
endpoints. The exact test conditions on n+ + n−and uses success probability 1/2 under the paired
null.
Centering and dose controls. The centering arm uses h′ = h + (¯s −v⊤h)v, where ¯s is the cali-
bration mean style projection across both styles. Its edit norm varies by request, so it is exploratory.
Single-dose additive controls use norm g = 8.181, double style uses 2g, and sham uses zero. Norm
verification checks the edit after the hook. The implementation names random_42 and random_43
refer to consecutive draws from one seed-42 generator, not separately seeded experiments. A final-
prompt-token edit can affect subsequent decoding through the cached computation, but it is not
repeatedly applied to every generated token.

C.1
Output budgets and replay
Local baseline harmful generation reaches the 128-token limit in 26/66 outputs and benign genera-
tion in 113/132. These denominators include original and both rewrite conditions for successfully
rewritten requests. Local opinion expansion reduces baseline truncation from 103/174 to 30/174.
Remaining truncation limits conclusions about complete task fulfillment and subtle degradation
even when refusal prefixes are identifiable. Some Qwen API tasks also retain truncation at the
longer budget.
For Gemini’s paraphrase-opinion baseline, 131/174 outputs are truncated at the initial API cap and
none after expansion. Even with the longer cap, 79/174 lack an unambiguous choice. A 24-request
uncached API replay exactly reproduces 10/24 texts but agrees on 23/24 coded endpoints. A local
16-request same-batch replay reproduces every output exactly. Cached-output analysis is repro-
ducible, while future API generations need not be identical.
