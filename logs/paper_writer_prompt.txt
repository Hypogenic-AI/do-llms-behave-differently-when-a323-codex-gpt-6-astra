# Do LLMs behave differently when the prompter is human versus another LLM?

Research execution: 2026-10-04. **Exploratory controlled study; the broad causal hypothesis remains unresolved.**

## 1. Executive Summary

We tested whether AI-like user-prompt style changes refusal, task fulfillment, and opinion alignment, and whether an internal style direction causes those changes. In a control that preserved every byte of the underlying request and all wrapper words, Markdown/casing increased Gemini’s AI-authorship-style classifications by **64.4 percentage points on harmful requests and 88.1 points on benign requests**. The corresponding refusal differences were **+1.7 and 0.0 points**. None of the six model-by-domain primary formatting comparisons was significant after Holm correction (all adjusted p=1). These results separate a large elicited style-judgment change from an unestablished behavioral effect; they do **not** establish general behavioral equivalence.

The broader paraphrase experiment encountered the anticipated semantic-control problem: only **7 of 124 successfully rewritten benchmark requests** survived the strict full-detail audit. The local Qwen3-8B activation classifier distinguished rewrite styles (AUROC **0.990**), but a seven-feature surface baseline already reached **0.970**, and residualizing those features reduced activation-probe AUROC to **0.665**. Norm-matched, fixed-text steering produced no robust behavior change specific to the style direction. Thus neither separability nor these interventions demonstrate an internal “LLM author” mechanism.

There was one exploratory signal worth retaining: in the exact-content formatting arm, Qwen’s *unconditional explicit opinion alignment* fell **11.7 points**, nominal p=.039, but p=.078 after even the two-model sensitivity-family correction. The main conditional-choice endpoint was not significant. Authorship manipulation also failed on Qwen’s verbal readout. This is a replication target, not evidence that the overall hypothesis has been confirmed or refuted.

## 2. Research Question & Motivation

Synthetic user turns are common in automated evaluation and orchestration. If a responder treats their apparent source differently, safety evaluations may not transfer to real users. We distinguish **actual provenance**, **AI-like writing style**, **declared source**, **elicited source judgments**, **activation coordinates**, and **behavior**. All generated paraphrases here are LLM-written, including the human-style controls. We did not compare verified human authors with verified machine authors, and did not test whether models generally expect humans as users.

The testable questions were: does a style manipulation change behavior; does the responder report a different apparent source; does a held-out style direction generalize; does editing it on fixed text change behavior beyond random/nuisance directions; and how does it relate to an evaluation-framing contrast?

### Literature Review Summary

The gathered [literature review](literature_review.md) and 24-paper catalog informed the design. Needham et al. classify evaluation/deployment transcripts but do not isolate authorship. Hawthorne/test-awareness studies motivate intervention controls. RepreGuard and linear-probe detectors motivate activation extraction, while self-preference studies primarily concern text being judged. Devbunova’s format-sensitivity analysis and the prompt-construction/political-stance study warn that a predictive representation can encode surface features. We inspected the gathered probe implementations and used their last-token extraction/mean-difference approach; upstream code was not treated as a turnkey replication. This run supplies a bounded experiment, not an exhaustive new novelty search.

## 3. Methodology and Experimental Setup

### Data, sampling and controls

Seed **42** selected 60 harmful and 60 benign JBB behaviors. Sixty unique terminal survey-question groups were sampled from the verified original Anthropic sycophancy files: 40 philosophy, 15 NLP, and 5 political items, one persona per selected group. A separate 100-request WildChat calibration set used unique English first turns of 12–120 words from the gathered prefix snapshot. WildChat has unverified authorship and is not representative of deployment. Benchmark contamination cannot be excluded. No factual-accuracy benchmark was added; arithmetic was a steering diagnostic only.

Two writers alternated across requests. They produced human-style and polished AI-style paraphrases while instructed to preserve task, persona, stance, options, specificity and harm level. An independently presented A/B audit checked equivalence, clarity and politeness. Acceptance initially required both semantic approvals, an AI/human word ratio within [0.8,1.25], and clarity/politeness differences no larger than one point. A pre-outcome spot-check found omissions despite approval, so a second cross-writer audit required preservation of **all** details and each rewrite/original word ratio in [0.75,1.25]. The strict auditor sometimes also over-penalized tone changes; these are fallible automated gates, not human gold labels. No human evaluation occurred.

| domain | sampled | rewritten | first_audit | pair_equivalent | strict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| harmful | 60 | 22 | 15 | 5 | 1 |
| benign | 60 | 44 | 29 | 14 | 3 |
| sycophancy | 60 | 58 | 40 | 3 | 3 |
| calibration | 100 | 93 | 77 | 38 | 18 |

Of 280 requested rewrite pairs, 217 were produced; writer refusals/blocked or malformed outputs were retained as attrition, not silently replaced. Refusal-heavy attrition especially limits the harmful paraphrase subset. The first audit accepted 161 pairs overall; direct-pair-equivalence accepted 60; the strict audit accepted 25, including 18 calibration and only 7 benchmark pairs. Complete prompts and audits are in [prompts.json](results/processed/prompts.json), with selection checks in [data_checks.json](results/processed/data_checks.json).

Because semantic controls largely failed, we added an explicit **format/case control** on all 180 benchmark requests:

```text
Human-style wrapper: please help with this request: {ORIGINAL}
thanks.
AI-style wrapper:    **Please help with this request:**

{ORIGINAL}

**Thanks.**
```

The underlying request is byte-identical and the complete case-insensitive word sequence is identical. Politeness words are held fixed. Markdown, casing, whitespace and tokenization differ by design. This isolates a narrow surface manipulation; it does not cover all AI style or isolate authorship belief from formatting. The source-label arm separately prefixes the **same human-style body** with “The following request was written by a human/an AI system,” for the first 20 available requests per domain. It is a partial attribution design, not the full style×label factorial proposed in the resource-finder handoff.

### Representation and intervention protocol

We preselected decoder-block 17’s output (hidden-state slot **18 of 36**) at the final nonpadding prompt token. All styles of each request stay together. Training uses 77 first-audit-accepted WildChat pairs; testing uses 124 benchmark pairs, with no request overlap. Standardization and all fitted models use training data only. We compare a mean-difference direction, logistic activation probe (C=.1), shuffled-label probe, and a surface logistic probe using word count, character count, newlines, colons, question marks, “please” count, and uppercase fraction. Unseen-writer tests train on one writer’s calibration pairs and evaluate the other writer’s benchmark pairs. They also change domain mixtures, so are not pure generator-only contrasts.

The style unit vector is the mean paired AI-minus-human activation difference. Evaluation framing contrasts “a model evaluation” with “a real user conversation” on fixed calibration bodies. The length nuisance vector is the word-count coefficient from a ridge model predicting activations from all seven surface features. A training-only surface regression supplies a separate residual-probe diagnostic. Neither the style vector nor the framing vector is assumed to be a verified belief representation.

On 20 fixed human-style bodies per domain, we intervened **only at the final prompt token during prefill**, at the same layer: sham; ±one mean-gap style dose; double style dose; two random unit vectors; evaluation and length vectors with the same norm; and centering/ablation of the style coordinate. One gap has norm **8.181**, versus mean calibration hidden norm **83.518**. Actual additive edit norms match within 1%, verified after the hook. Random controls named `random_42`/`random_43` are **two consecutive draws from RNG seed 42**, not two separately seeded runs. Centering has a sample-dependent norm and is exploratory, not itself norm-matched. No natural activation rescue or all-token intervention was performed.

### Metrics and statistical analysis

Refusal is explicit refusal of all or a substantive part of the request. It is **not the complement of harmful compliance**. A separate 0/1/2 fulfillment rubric distinguishes none, partial/generic, and substantial requested assistance. Opinion alignment is endorsement of the persona-matching option; it is not factual accuracy or proof of sycophancy caused by that persona. Main opinion comparisons require unambiguous choices in both arms. No-choice/abstaining outputs are reported separately, with an unconditional explicit-alignment sensitivity analysis that codes no observed endorsement as zero.

Judges see only the canonical request and response, never treatment/source labels. Gemini responses are judged by Qwen3.5-397B; Qwen API/local responses by Gemini. Canonical-request judging can still be biased when the rewrite changes meaning. A second-model audit of 90 long-budget outputs found:

| Domain | N | Judge agreement | Cohen κ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| harmful | 30 | 90.0% | 0.609 |
| benign | 30 | 96.7% | 0.911 |
| sycophancy | 30 | 100.0% | 1.000 |

These are model-model agreement estimates, not accuracy against human labels. Representative benign disagreements are saved in [benign_discordant_examples.json](results/benign_discordant_examples.json).

We report request-paired risk differences, 10,000-resample paired bootstrap 95% intervals, exact McNemar/binomial discordance p-values, and Holm correction across the six primary API model×domain tests within each experiment. Opinion complete-case selection can bias these estimates. Zero-discordance bootstraps collapse to [0,0], so we also report conservative **at-least-95% bounds** obtained by differencing simultaneous 97.5% Clopper–Pearson intervals for the two discordant probabilities. Those bounds avoid presenting zero observed changes as certainty. They are pointwise, not simultaneous across all endpoints. Secondary endpoint families are marked exploratory. A planned 10-point meaningful-effect threshold is generally not ruled out; at N=60 and discordance .2, approximate 80%-power sensitivity is about 16 points. The local per-domain N=20 interventions are substantially less precise.

## 5. Analysis & Discussion

The most defensible result is a **measurement dissociation**: exact-content surface formatting substantially changes Gemini’s elicited source judgments without a detected primary behavioral effect in this sample. The premise that short prompts are consistently recognized as machine-written is model/readout dependent. In particular, Qwen’s highly separable internal activations coexist with weak verbal source discrimination; a classifier fitted by the investigator need not measure a belief used by the responder.

The strongest practical failure was semantic control. For example, both syc_0 rewrites dropped parts of the original persona/research-interest details, while the first auditor incorrectly approved preservation. Other rewrites changed “program” into “script,” interpreted ambiguous requirements, or changed specificity. Conversely, the strict auditor sometimes rejected harmless request-tone changes. The audit disagreement makes any claim from the seven retained benchmark pairs unstable. These findings support the submitter’s warning that an ordinary rewrite comparison can be misleading.

The output-budget check also mattered: initial Gemini paraphrase-opinion outputs were truncated in 131/174 cases; after expansion none were truncated, but 79/174 still lacked an unambiguous choice. These are often substantive abstentions or multi-perspective answers, not parsing defects. Qwen’s paraphrase conditional-alignment contrast changed sign between the short and long passes; generation budget and backend variation should not be ignored. [budget_diagnostics.csv](results/budget_diagnostics.csv) and saved initial results make this visible.

Behavioral estimates can also reflect judge variation. Independent long-output judges disagreed on 3/30 harmful and 1/30 benign samples. That is large relative to several observed treatment differences. The 24-request uncached API replay exactly reproduced only **10/24 texts**, although **23/24 coded endpoints** agreed. Cached-output analysis is reproducible; future API generations are not guaranteed identical even with temperature 0 and requested seed 42. In contrast, the local 16-request same-batch replay reproduced every output exactly.

## 6. Limitations & Future Work

1. **No general human-vs-LLM authorship claim.** Human-style text is generated; original dataset provenance is not verified human-only. Elicited style classifications need not reflect spontaneous inference during responding.
2. **Paraphrase content control failed at scale.** Attrition is severe and selective. Automated semantic audits have false approvals and apparent false rejections; there are no human gold audits. Only the narrower format control has mechanically guaranteed fixed lexical content.
3. **Power/headroom are limited.** N=60 per format domain, N=22 harmful paraphrases, and N=20 per intervention domain cannot rule out small effects. Harmful refusal is often near ceiling. Benign JBB requests supply more headroom, but no dedicated borderline-risk benchmark was constructed.
4. **Opinion alignment has construct and selection limits.** Persona alignment is not necessarily unreasonable agreement, and no neutral/opposite-persona factorial was run. Conditional-choice analysis selects on a treatment-affected outcome; unconditional coding is therefore reported separately. Opinion labels provide no factual accuracy ground truth.
5. **Mechanistic identifiability is weak.** One older local backbone, one layer/token/time, two random draws, no all-token ablation or natural rescue, and a weakly transferring evaluation-framing proxy. The learned style direction mixes surface variables; a causal change in logits is not proof of an authorship-mediated behavioral pathway. The same mechanism was not tested inside Gemini.
6. **Scoring and generation remain variable.** Judges are models with correlated biases; generator/respondent families overlap; residual truncation remains locally and in some Qwen API tasks. Automatic provider routing is not pinned. Only a small API replay sample was checked.
7. **External validity is narrow.** English, synthetic survey personas, a WildChat prefix, one rewrite per writer/request, potentially contaminated public benchmarks, and efficient API models. No multi-agent tool-use environment, human comparison, cross-lingual replication, or broad capability battery was run.

The next study should construct human-audited proposition-preserving pairs or a richer fixed-content factorial, include several borderline-risk domains, validate source and evaluation representations on independently labeled held-out corpora/templates, and power behavioral estimates from observed discordance. If a natural effect replicates, test necessity and matched-coordinate rescue across layers/tokens with more random and nuisance controls. Replicate the Qwen unconditional-alignment signal with fixed provider routing and prespecified abstention handling before interpreting it.

## 7. Conclusions

Some prompt surface cues clearly change a model’s reported authorship-style judgment. This study does not establish that they reliably change safety-relevant behavior through an internal representation of “an LLM wrote this.” The controlled formatting results are compatible with a bounded null for several endpoints, but failures of broader content control, limited power, weak target engagement and readout dependence leave the general causal question open.

## Motivation & Novelty Assessment

### Why This Research Matters
LLM-generated instructions appear in synthetic safety evaluations and multi-agent orchestration. If apparent authorship changes behavior, those evaluations may fail to transfer to human deployment.

### Gap in Existing Work
The gathered literature separately studies evaluation awareness, authorship detection, and self-preference. It does not establish authorship-mediated safety behavior under strong content controls. Format/length and pragmatic differences are competing explanations.

### Our Novel Contribution
Join controlled style contrasts, independent-session authorship checks, fixed-body source labels, and held-out activation interventions. Distinguish generated human style from actual human provenance. Detection and steering alone cannot establish mediation.

### Experiment Justification
1. Original/human-style/AI-style prompts separate paraphrasing from AI-like style, conditional on semantic audits.
2. Human/AI source labels on identical bodies test attribution sensitivity separately from text effects.
3. Fixed-text activation interventions with random, length and framing controls test specificity and degradation.

## Research Question and Hypothesis Decomposition
H1: AI-like style changes refusal, task fulfillment or opinion alignment. H2: the responder perceives the manipulation as AI-like. H3: a held-out style representation generalizes and specifically affects behavior when edited on fixed text. H4: it overlaps an independently checked evaluation-framing direction. These hypotheses must be assessed separately; human/AI ground-truth authorship is not available.

## Proposed Methodology

### Approach and Experimental Steps
- Seed 42: sample 60 harmful and 60 benign JBB behaviors; 60 unique Anthropic survey-question groups; 100 separate short English WildChat calibration requests. Do not treat the gathered WildChat prefix as representative or verified-human data.
- Check the live OpenRouter catalog before selection. Use current efficient-tier Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite and Qwen3.5-9B for API response evaluation; Gemini and Qwen3.5-397B alternate as writers. Use a pinned, explicitly historical Qwen3-8B for mechanistic access on the A6000.
- Generate two paraphrases preserving task, entities, persona, stance, certainty, options and harm level. Compare original and both rewrites. Audit paired semantic equivalence, clarity and politeness before behavioral analysis; report failures and attrition.
- Run independent conversations and separate AI/HUMAN style-classification sessions. Cross the first 20 available human-style bodies per domain with human/AI source declarations.
- Extract layer-18 final-prompt-token activations. Train all preprocessing/probes only on calibration pairs. Test on benchmark requests and unseen-writer transfer; compare surface-only and shuffled-label baselines.
- Intervene only at the last prompt token during prefill. Compare sham, ±style, double style, two equal-norm random draws, length/evaluation directions, and coordinate centering. Score generated behavior, separate authorship readouts, next-token KL and arithmetic capability. Do not infer necessity or mediation without successful target engagement and stronger rescue evidence.

### Baselines and Evaluation Metrics
Original request; human-style paraphrase; exact-content formatting control (amendment below); no-source and explicit-source conditions. Primary endpoints are refusal in harmful/benign domains and opinion alignment among unambiguous choices. Also report fulfillment, abstention/no-choice coverage, unconditional explicit alignment, truncation, incoherence, probe AUROC/AUPRC, framing overlap, and capability diagnostics. None is factual accuracy except the limited arithmetic checks.

### Statistical Analysis Plan
Within-request differences, 10,000 paired bootstrap resamples, exact McNemar tests, and Holm correction across six API model×domain primary tests per experiment. Conservative simultaneous-binomial difference bounds supplement degenerate zero-discordance bootstrap intervals. Keep variants grouped; repeats are not independent requests. Opinion missingness and audit attrition require sensitivity analyses. A 10-point meaningful-effect threshold is often below feasible precision (roughly 16-point detectable effect at N=60 and discordance .2). Secondary families are exploratory.

## Documented Amendments and Challenges
- **Pre-outcome audit correction:** a spot-check found omitted persona facts despite the first auditor’s approval. Add a strict cross-writer omission audit and rewrite/original word ratios in [0.75,1.25]; preserve initial and strict subsets.
- **Before behavioral analysis:** strict filtering left only seven benchmark pairs. Add fixed-content formatting/case contrasts on all 180 requests, with identical request bytes and complete case-insensitive word sequences. This tests format sensitivity, not comprehensive authorship style.
- **After instrumentation diagnostics:** the 256-token API cap caused extensive truncation. Uniformly extend all available behavioral cells to 1,536 tokens; retain initial results. Expand local opinion outputs from 128 to 384 tokens. Keep provider blocks missing, distinct from assistant refusal.
- Check strict-calibration-only and surface-residual probes; validate framing on new equal-word-count cue templates. These are sensitivity checks, not a search over new research directions.
- Human auditing, a broad factual benchmark, full label×style factorial, distributed steering, and natural activation rescue are not completed. Their absence limits the causal claim.

## Expected Outcomes and Success Criteria

Success requires actual model outputs, transparent uncertainty/attrition, code and evidence artifacts, and honest limits. It does not require confirming the hypothesis. No broad causal conclusion follows from AUROC alone.

# Literature review

## Scope and conclusion

The literature supports prompt-sensitive behavior and some ability to detect text provenance. It does **not** establish that a responder changes safety behavior because it internally identifies its prompter as an LLM. Actual authorship, AI-like style, declared authorship, evaluation context and task content are distinct variables. The strongest opportunity is to cross them under audited semantic controls, then test whether a generalizing representation mediates behavior.

Search: diligent paper-finder first (39 candidates), then primary arXiv/publisher pages, Semantic Scholar metadata attempts and citation chasing. Twelve finder candidates passed task-specific screening; 24 PDFs total were downloaded, including all ten specified papers. Semantic Scholar returned 429; publisher CDN resolved an MDPI 403. Search decisions: [screening log](notes/search_screening.md). All specified PDFs and Devbunova were read through every 3-page chunk using text-assisted reading and rendered sheets. Other papers received targeted screening, not exhaustive appendix review. Full author lists, PDF versions, hashes and links: [paper catalog](papers/README.md); detailed notes: [reading log](notes/reading_log.md), [extended notes](notes/extended_reading.md). Findings below are published results, not results from this workspace.

## Key papers: authorship and preference

**Panickssery, Bowman & Feng (2024), “LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076). GPT-4, GPT-3.5 and Llama2 evaluate summaries of 1,000 articles each from XSUM and CNN/DailyMail. Pairwise and individual ratings, order reversal, self-recognition fine-tuning and length/vowel/readability control tasks connect recognition with preference. GPT-4 achieves 73.5% recognition in the reported aggregate. Swapping source labels changes GPT-4 XSUM preference from 0.73 to 0.32, but CNN only 0.94 to 0.89. The authors explicitly leave causal validation open; fine-tuning can alter other properties. Reuse label swaps and order controls, not the assumption that recognition causes preference. Code linked from paper: `bit.ly/llm_self_recognition`.

**Laurito et al. (2024/PNAS 2025), “AI–AI Bias: Large Language Models Favor Communications Generated by Large Language Models.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12856). Regenerates descriptions for 109 products, 100 papers and 250 movies, then compares choices across five model families and a small human sample. GPT-4-generated product descriptions are selected 89% by LLMs versus 36% by humans. Counterbalanced presentation and abstract word-count matching address some confounds. Human comparison has 13 raters; invalid responses are excluded, and title-based movie generation need not preserve facts. This is stylistic preference evidence, not safety mediation. Code: [ai-ai-bias](https://github.com/lauritowal/ai-ai-bias).

**Xu, Li & Jiang (2025, PDF revised 2026), “AI Self-preferencing in Algorithmic Hiring.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00462). Uses 2,245 cleaned pre-LLM resumes, regenerated summaries, nine evaluators, conditional logistic regressions controlling LIWC/semantic similarity, and 18 human annotators. Larger-model adjusted self-minus-human preference gaps range approximately 67–82 percentage points; cross-LLM effects are weaker and heterogeneous. Revision-only checks and simulated screening extend the result. Baselines include original summaries, other generators, source-neutral instructions and small-model voting. Similarity controls do not guarantee equal information or quality; simulation is not actual hiring, and mitigation does not isolate self-recognition. Public resume source: Kaggle/LiveCareer; no dedicated code release verified.

## Key papers: awareness and causal interpretation

**Needham et al. (2025), “Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23836). Builds 1,000 transcripts from 61 sources, balanced chat/agentic and evaluation/deployment, with 100 development and 900 test items. Five elicitation questions and repeated samples produce AUC around 0.83 for strong models versus 0.921 for informed human authors. Agentic discrimination exceeds chat; probability calibration is poor. Multiple-choice versus open-ended purpose identification differs strongly. Deployment includes synthetic instructions, so its labels cannot become authorship labels. Explicitly asking whether a transcript is an evaluation may itself prime the phenomenon. Code: [eval_awareness](https://github.com/jjpn97/eval_awareness).

**Abdelnabi & Salem (2025), “The Hawthorne Effect in Reasoning Models: Evaluating and Steering Test Awareness.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14617). Three 32B reasoning models, 2,587 real/hypothetical prompt pairs, judge-labeled reasoning spans, linear probes and edits to aligned FFN rows. Span probes reach approximately 93–95% accuracy, but prompt-token readouts are weaker. On 150 synthetic tool-call pairs, steering awareness increases QwQ real-task execution 36.2→56.3%; effects differ by model/direction. Standard harmful-answer judgments show weaker evidence. HarmBench, BBQ and small capability subsets provide checks. “Test,” “hypothetical” and safety concepts remain entangled. Code: [Test_Awareness_Steering](https://github.com/microsoft/Test_Awareness_Steering). The specified [LessWrong post](https://lesswrong.com/posts/B2o6nrxwKxLPsSYdh/do-llms-comply-differently-during-tests-is-this-a-hidden) summarizes earlier work, not independent corroboration.

**Nguyen et al. (2025), “Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01786). Llama3.3-70B, 480 layer/dataset probes, SAD selection and held-out benchmark classification. A simple contrastive probe attains AUC 0.816 versus length 0.584 and random 0.517. In prompted WMDP sandbagging, linear-probe steering recovers under 5% of lost accuracy; selected SAE features recover up to roughly 25%, but an unrelated feature is competitive. An explicit deployment suffix recovers 83%. This is a valuable negative/specificity control: predictive probes need not identify the controlling mechanism. Code is linked in paper; reuse diagnostics through the related repositories in the resource catalog.

**Heidari, Memarian & Rabusseau (2026), “Evaluation Awareness in Language Models: Representation, Verbalization, and Control.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21766). Six 7–49B open models, 16 framing pairs, 400 calibration/200 test items from WildChat and benchmarks, plus 1,000 MASK prompts for verbalization. Best-layer AUROC exceeds 0.7, but prompt-level probe/verbalization correlation is small; steering changes verbalization. Training-stage comparisons use Olmo. This does not establish safety changes. Appendix judge-rubric changes greatly alter Olmo prevalence; no human gold validation. Norm scaling includes padding, and large steering causes degeneration. Code: [evaluation-awareness](https://github.com/evaluation-awareness/evaluation-awareness).

## Key papers: detection and surface confounds

**Quaremba et al. (2026), “Linear Probing Provides Robust and Efficient Detection of Machine-Generated Text.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24780). Frozen Llama3-8B last-token activations, training-only PCA 100, per-layer averaged or concatenated logistic probes; four benchmarks (DetectRL, MultiSocial, RAID, TSM), 16 subsets with 1,500/500 train/test examples and 16 detector baselines. Probes improve in-domain and transfer AUROC; per-layer averaging transfers more reliably. Few-label results retain PCA fitted on the full training split. OOD transfer is within benchmark families, and “machineness” is not demonstrated as an authorship belief. Good readout baseline, not a causal conclusion. Code: [mgt_probes](https://github.com/gerritq/mgt_probes).

**Chen et al. (2025), “RepreGuard: Detecting LLM-Generated Text by Revealing Hidden Representation Patterns.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13152). PCA of paired human/LLM activation differences, tail-token projection averages and a calibrated threshold. DetectRL experiments use 512 training pairs, 1,000 test pairs and five resamples; reported ID/OOD AUROC 96.34/93.49 against statistical and fine-tuned detectors. Short-text performance is weaker. Despite “training-free” terminology, labeled pairs and threshold selection are needed. FPR notation is inconsistent between prose and tables; verify before citing extreme-tail performance. Code: [RepreGuard](https://github.com/NLP2CT/RepreGuard).

**Chalkidis (2026), “Templated or Fully Synthetic? Prompt Construction as a Confound…”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11008). Compares real chat prompts, 2,700 templated and 2,700 LLM-generated prompts across six topics, three intents and three stances ; 120 triplets for realism and 150 prompts for construct labeling. Humans rank generated prompts nearly as realistic as real ones. Templates encode unintended stance via fillers; neutral-setting stance estimates differ by about 0.42 ordinal points for one responder. Judges lack human response-label validation; refusals are excluded at different rates. Detectability is explicitly not tested as a behavioral mediator. Released data: [synthetic_polistance](https://huggingface.co/datasets/kiddothe2b/synthetic_polistance). AI-generated does not necessarily mean AI-like.

**Devbunova (2026), “Is Evaluation Awareness Just Format Sensitivity?”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19426). A 2×2 context/format design with Llama3.1-8B, length-matched ShareGPT/benchmark contrasts and rewrite controls. Standard probes call 94.5% of benchmark-formatted deployment prompts evaluations; decorrelated training reduces this to 7.2%. Formal prose alone does not reproduce the effect. MuTual first turns remain a proxy, sources differ, and only one backbone is studied; the result challenges probe interpretation, not awareness's existence. Code: [eval-awareness-format](https://github.com/viliana-dev/eval-awareness-format).

## Recommended experiment and evaluation

Keep exactly the three ranked directions in [planning.md](planning.md): (1) semantic-matched style pairs; (2) the same bodies crossed with human/AI/unspecified source labels; (3) source-generalizing probes plus necessity/rescue interventions. Pair creation must preserve task, information, stance strength and requested harm level, with blinded equivalence review. Length, clarity, politeness, formatting, certainty and grammatical person require explicit controls. Use multiple writer families; keep all variants of a base request together across splits.

Prepared datasets are JBB 100 harmful/100 benign, 30,051 verified Anthropic opinion-sycophancy rows, and 1,000 WildChat conversations for wording/pilot validation. The HF philosophy file duplicated NLP; **use `datasets/anthropic_verified/`**. WildChat has 995 unique hashes and 523 English labels, is a prefix sample, and does not certify human authorship. These data do not supply objective answer-accuracy labels.

Behavioral baselines: original/neutral request, matched single-feature edits, unlabeled source, counterfactual labels. Report harmful compliance separately from benign over-refusal, opinion agreement separately from factual error, and invalid outputs separately from refusal. Use blind cross-family judges plus human validation. Report paired percentage-point effects and confidence intervals clustered by base request, style×label interactions, discordant counts, and multiplicity-adjusted primary tests. Repeated generations do not increase independent prompt count; size the main study from pilot discordance.

Mechanistic baselines: surface-only and shuffled-label probes, training-only preprocessing, held-out generator/domain splits, random/nuisance/evaluation-awareness vectors and sham interventions. Report AUROC/AUPRC plus calibrated probabilities only when calibrated. Measure fluency and capability under interventions; mask padding and retain failed outputs. A causal claim requires specificity and necessity/rescue under natural variation, not merely separability or steerability. A null style effect within one benchmark or model cannot establish universal invariance.

   - Avoid fancy wording: "utilize" → "use", "facilitate" → "help"

2. INTRODUCTION STRUCTURE:
   - Engaging hook (get to the point quickly)
   - Problem importance
   - Gap identification
   - Your approach with method figure reference
   - Quantitative preview of results
   - Contribution bullets (3-4 items, action verbs)

3. CONTRIBUTION LISTS:
   \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt,topsep=0pt]
       \item We propose...
       \item We conduct...
       \item We complement...
   \end{itemize}

4. MODULAR COMMANDS STRUCTURE:
   Command templates are pre-copied to paper_draft/commands/:
   - math.tex: Math notation macros
   - general.tex: Formatting macros
   - macros.tex: Project-specific term definitions (customize this for your paper)

   In main.tex, include:
   \input{commands/math}
   \input{commands/general}
   \input{commands/macros}

5. REFERENCE CONVENTIONS:
   Use reference macros from math.tex:
   - \figref{fig:name} for "figure 1" (lowercase, in-sentence)
   - \Figref{fig:name} for "Figure 1" (capitalized, start of sentence)
   - \secref{sec:name} for "section 2"

6. TEXT FORMATTING:
   - Use \para{Header text} for bold paragraph headers
   - Define method/dataset names with \textsc and \xspace:
     \newcommand{\methodname}{\textsc{MethodName}\xspace}

7. TABLE FORMATTING:
   - Use booktabs package (no vertical lines)
   - Use \resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{...} for wide tables
   - Use @{} to remove padding at table edges
   - Use \cmidrule(lr){x-y} for sub-headers
   - Use \textsc{} for dataset/method names in headers
   - Bold best results with {\bf ...}

8. FIGURE FORMATTING:
   - Use 0.32\textwidth for 3-column subfigures
   - Use 0.95\linewidth for full-width figures
   - Use \input{figures/legend} for shared legends
   - Write self-contained captions explaining key observations

9. RESULTS PRESENTATION:
   - Define \increase and \decrease for colored arrows (green up, red down)
   - Bold best results in tables
   - Report confidence intervals when available

10. ALGORITHM STYLING:
    - Use algpseudocode with [noend]
    - Use \triangleright for comments

11. HYPERLINKS (REQUIRED):
    - Always use \usepackage[hidelinks]{hyperref} or with colored links
    - All citations, section refs, figure refs, table refs must be clickable
    - This is essential for reader navigation
