## Completed reading notes

Chalkidis (2608.11008), chunks 1–4 (pp.1–12): two 2,700-prompt construction arms across six topics, three intents and three stances. 120 triplets for realism ranking; 150 prompts for construct detection. LLM-generated does not mean detectably nonhuman: human realness ranks 1.25 real vs 1.26 generated; templated 1.53. Templates introduce stance via fillers; neutral setting average gap .42 ordinal points, with topic dependence. Response judges have no human validation; dropped refusal rates differ (5% vs 2%). This is evidence of construction confounding, not causal authorship recognition.

Chalkidis chunks 5–8 (pp.13–24): explicitly acknowledges detectability not tested as mediator; one generator; API-only setting; neutral-scale averaging and topic dependence. Full generation/annotation rubrics supplied. Human raters inspect triplets together but LLM raters use independent calls, so cross-rater contrast mixes presentation design. 80 unique real prompts occupy 120 slots, versus 120 distinct prompts in each synthetic arm. Use blinded equivalence and perceived-source checks in separate sessions.

Chalkidis chunks 9–11 (pp.25–33): Grok comparison replicates direction with smaller mean neutral gap .36; ensemble excludes 43/69 no-majority cases (2%/3%). Appendix includes exact fillers, template lists, UI screenshots, per-setting tables. Recognition remains an untested explanation. All 11 chunk sheets inspected alongside text.

Xu et al. (2509.00462), chunks 1–6 (pp.1–18): 2,245 cleaned pre-LLM resumes, nine evaluators; regenerate only summary, length target 32–84 words, randomize pair order. Conditional logistic regressions control LIWC, BERTScore, ROUGE-L; 18 human raters, 30 substantive comparisons each across six conditions. Reported 67–82% figures are preference probability DIFFERENCES (percentage points), not raw selection probabilities. Residual quality confounding remains: neither similarity scores nor regression guarantee identical meaning.

Xu chunks 7–16 (pp.19–46): cross-LLM effects smaller and heterogeneous; human-annotation cross-LLM CIs include zero. Revision robustness reduces several effects. Simulated screening samples five duplicated profiles, selects four of ten; not real hiring outcomes. System instruction and smaller-model voting reduce bias but do not isolate recognition. Appendix generation prompt actually says 30–80 words (main text implies empirical quartiles). Human/AI examples visibly differ in information selection, warranting strict proposition audits. All 16 chunks inspected including regression tables and annotation UI.

Heidari et al. (2608.21766), all ten chunks inspected with text: C1 pp1–3: 16 contrast pairs, six open models, separate representation/verbalization/control. C2 pp4–6: AUROC above .7 at best layer, low per-prompt verbalization correlation; intervention chiefly changes verbalization. C3 pp7–9: pre/post-training comparison, surface confounds acknowledged, references. C4 pp10–12: 400 calibration/200 test, 1,000 MASK prompts, three generations, judge rubric. C5 pp13–15: alternate rubric greatly changes Olmo prevalence; no human gold standard. C6 pp16–18: random controls and threshold-transfer limitations. C7 pp19–21: response-readout correlations remain low. C8 pp22–24: norm scaling includes padding; empty generations excluded; best-layer mean+SD selection is exploratory. C9 pp25–27: visual dose-response curves show nonlinear strength and degeneration; direction alignment across training. C10 pp28–30: agreement heatmaps and related work reinforce that separability, expression and causal behavior are distinct. Code: evaluation-awareness/evaluation-awareness.

Quaremba et al. (2608.24780), eight chunks: C1 pp1–3 latent detection question, not behavior. C2 pp4–6 logistic LLP/CLP, PCA100 train-only, 1,500/500 balanced splits for 16 subsets. C3 pp7–9 OOD transfer within benchmark, 10–100 label efficiency (PCA still uses training split), limitations acknowledge confounded directions. C4 pp10–12 and C5 pp13–15 references and representation metrics. C6 pp16–18 multilingual/task/attack datasets and 16 detector baselines. C7 pp19–21 full heatmaps and length tests; LLP outperforms CLP on transfer. C8 pp22–24 three-class EditLens macro-F1 .848 LLP, no-PCA and pooling ablations; projection calibration transform deserves code verification. Repo gerritq/mgt_probes. All chunk sheets inspected with text.

Chen et al. (2508.13152), seven chunks: C1 pp1–3 hidden-pattern detector; C2 pp4–6 paired differences/PCA, average tail-token projections, Youden threshold, DetectRL 512 train pairs/1,000 test pairs, five resamples. C3 pp7–9 ID96.34/OOD93.49 AUROC; surrogate and token-tail ablations. C4 pp10–12 short prompts weaker, paraphrase tests, A100 resource costs. C5 pp13–15 acknowledgments/references. C6 pp16–18 POS/token analysis, 2025 Reddit validation and synthetic pretraining sensitivity. C7 p19 precision-tail degradation and attack plots. FPR notation is ambiguous (text says .01%, tables @.01), so do not reuse low-FPR claims without code verification. Source recognition is not established by detector accuracy. All chunks inspected with text.

Needham et al. (2505.23836): all 11 chunk sheets visually checked after the full text pass logged in notes/reading_log.md. C8 confirms synthetic commit-derived deployment prompts, so deployment is not a human-author label. C11 confirms compute ~1B tokens and non-independent repeated probe-question measurements. Probe-question elicitation is itself an evaluation cue.

Abdelnabi & Salem (2505.14617): all seven chunk sheets checked, supplementing per-chunk text notes. C3 Table4 confirms direction-dependent tool-call effects; baseline differs from capability-ablation Table5. C5 algorithm selects best layer on reported test set: future design needs separate validation. C6–7 qualitative cases show scenario recognition, test awareness and safety reasoning intertwined. No tool execution was performed in this resource phase.

Nguyen et al. (2507.01786): six chunk sheets checked with text notes. C2 length and special-character baselines underperform selected probe, but C3–6 show probe-steering recovery <5%, unrelated SAE feature competitive, deployment suffix much stronger. Synthetic Alpaca is deployment control. Readout predictive success is not sufficient intervention specificity.

Panickssery et al. (2404.13076), seven chunks: C1 definition and XSUM/CNN 1,000 each; C2 fine-tuning correlation with generation-quality check; C3 explicitly says causal hypothesis unvalidated. C4 exact generation constraints (XSUM30 words/CNN3–4 highlights), controls for length/vowels/readability. C5 pairwise prompting and source-label swaps; C6–7 individual score tables reveal strong response biases. Average across both orders rather than dropping inconsistent pairs. All chunks inspected alongside text.

Laurito et al. (2407.12856), three chunks: C1 109 products/100 scientific papers/250 movies, paired regenerated descriptions. C2 order bias tables, human comparison13 raters, invalid outputs excluded (up to ~30%); GPT4-written product pitches selected89% by models vs36% by humans. C3 explicitly leaves mechanism for future work; content quality/factual fidelity not perfectly matched (movies generated from title/year). All chunk sheets checked with text notes.

Devbunova (2603.19426), all four chunks (10pp) inspected visually and text: C1 2x2 context/format using Llama3.1-8B, ShareGPT, MuTual and benchmarks. C2 standard probe calls94.5% benchmark-rewritten deployment prompts evaluation, paired-training7.2%; length matching only primary contrast, n1076. C3 standardization train-only, L2C1, layer sweeps, routing and rewrite controls; both probes flag0% of tested safety benchmark prompts. C4 completes formal free-form rewrite rubric. Caveat: source/task and MuTual first-turn proxy remain confounded; grouped paired splits are essential. This does not prove absence of evaluation awareness. Repo viliana-dev/eval-awareness-format.
